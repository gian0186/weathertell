Forecast/Glossary/Fog

weather term

Fog

A cloud that forms at ground level, reducing visibility to less than roughly one kilometre.

Fog forms when air near the ground cools to its dew point, or when moisture is added to the air faster than it can be dispersed. Radiation fog, the most common type, forms on clear, calm nights as the ground cools rapidly and chills the air just above it, and typically clears within a few hours of sunrise as the sun warms the ground back up.

Fog or mist?

Fog and mist are both tiny water droplets floating in the air; the difference is how far you can see. By the international definition used by weather services, it is fog when visibility is below 1 kilometer (about 0.6 miles), and mist when you can see 1 kilometer or more. Dense fog, with visibility of a few hundred meters or less, is a real hazard on the roads; the US National Weather Service issues a dense fog advisory when visibility drops to a quarter mile (about 400 meters) or less.

How fog forms

Fog forms when the air close to the ground becomes saturated: either the air cools down to its dew point, or moisture is added until it reaches saturation. Then the water vapor condenses into tiny droplets. Fog is essentially a cloud at ground level.

The main types of fog

TypeHow it formsWhere and when
Radiation fogThe ground cools quickly on clear, calm nights and cools the air above itValleys and low-lying land, especially in autumn and winter; usually clears in the morning
Advection fogWarm, moist air moves over a cold surfaceCoasts, such as San Francisco in summer when moist ocean air crosses cold coastal water
Sea fogA form of advection fog over the seaCoastal areas in spring and early summer, when the sea is still cold
Freezing fogFog at temperatures below freezing; droplets freeze onto surfacesCold winter mornings; can leave rime on trees and make roads icy

Driving in fog

Slow down and leave much more space than usual, so you can stop within the distance you can see. Use low-beam headlights and fog lights if you have them; high beams reflect off the fog and make it harder to see. Switch off rear fog lights again when visibility improves, as they can dazzle other drivers. Watch out for patches of dense fog in low-lying areas, near rivers and on bridges, where conditions can change within seconds.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between fog and mist?
It is fog when visibility drops below 1 kilometer (about 0.6 miles), and mist when you can still see 1 kilometer or more.

Why does fog form at night?
On clear, calm nights the ground loses heat quickly and cools the air above it. When that air cools to its dew point, the moisture condenses into fog.

When does fog clear?
Radiation fog usually clears within a few hours after sunrise as the sun warms the ground. Advection fog along coasts can last much longer, sometimes all day.
