weather term

Barometric Pressure

The weight of the atmosphere pressing down at a given location, measured in units such as hectopascals or inches of mercury.

Falling pressure usually signals that a low-pressure system, and the unsettled, wetter weather that comes with it, is approaching. Rising pressure, by contrast, typically points toward calmer, clearer conditions. Meteorologists track pressure changes over time rather than a single reading, since the rate of change often says more about incoming weather than the absolute number itself.

Normal barometric pressure and units

At sea level, the average air pressure is 1013.25 hPa. That same pressure can be written in several units:

UnitAverage at sea level
Hectopascals (hPa) or millibars (mb)1013.25
Inches of mercury (inHg)29.92
Millimeters of mercury (mmHg)760
Kilopascals (kPa)101.3
In everyday weather, sea-level pressure usually stays between about 980 and 1040 hPa (28.9 to 30.7 inHg).

Station pressure vs. sea-level pressure

Air pressure drops with height: near sea level by about 1 hPa for every 8 meters (roughly 1 inHg per 1,000 feet). In Denver, at about 1,600 meters (5,280 feet), the actual air pressure is around 840 hPa. To be able to compare places, weather services convert the measured station pressure to what it would be at sea level. The pressure in a weather forecast or on a weather map is almost always this sea-level pressure; a barometer at home may show the station pressure unless it has been adjusted for your altitude.

What rising and falling pressure mean

The trend tells you more than a single reading. Falling pressure usually means a low-pressure system is approaching, with more cloud, rain and wind; a fall of more than about 3.5 hPa (0.1 inHg) in three hours is considered rapid and often signals strong wind. Rising pressure usually means a high is building and the weather is improving. Steady pressure means little change. Read more about low-pressure systems and high-pressure systems.

Frequently asked questions

What is normal barometric pressure?
About 1013 hPa, 29.92 inHg or 760 mmHg at sea level. Day-to-day values usually range from about 980 to 1040 hPa (28.9 to 30.7 inHg).

Is high or low barometric pressure better?
High pressure usually brings dry, settled weather; low pressure usually brings clouds, rain and wind.

What does a falling barometer mean?
Usually that a low-pressure system is approaching, with worsening weather. A rapid fall, of more than about 3.5 hPa in three hours, often means strong wind is on the way.
