weather term

High-Pressure System

An area where atmospheric pressure is higher than its surroundings, usually bringing settled, drier weather.

Air sinks and spreads outward within a high-pressure system, which suppresses cloud formation and typically brings calmer, clearer skies than a low-pressure system. In summer this often means warm, sunny weather, while in winter a persistent high can trap cold air near the ground, sometimes leading to fog or frost that lingers for days.

How high is high?

Average sea-level pressure is 1013 hPa (29.92 inHg). Most high-pressure systems have a central pressure of about 1020 to 1040 hPa (30.1 to 30.7 inHg). Strong winter highs over Siberia, Canada and the northern US can go higher: the highest sea-level pressure ever measured was 1084.8 hPa (32.03 inHg) in Agata, Siberia, on December 31, 1968.

Why highs bring settled weather

In a high, air sinks from higher up and spreads out near the ground. Sinking air warms and dries out, which dissolves clouds, so highs usually bring dry weather with little wind. Around the center, the air turns clockwise in the Northern Hemisphere and counterclockwise in the Southern Hemisphere. Because the isobars are usually far apart, the wind near the center is light.

Summer heat, winter fog and cold

What a high brings depends on the season. In summer, long days of sunshine under a high can build up a heat wave, especially when the high stalls; a strong, persistent high is often called a heat dome. In winter the opposite can happen: clear, calm nights let the ground cool quickly, cold air gets trapped near the surface under warmer air above, and you can get days of frost, fog or low gray cloud, while it is sunny and much milder on the mountains.

Blocking highs

Sometimes a high stalls for a week or more and pushes weather systems around it. Such a blocking high can bring a long dry spell, a heat wave or a cold snap, depending on the season and where the air is coming from. It also changes the path of the jet stream, which has to bend around it.

Frequently asked questions

What weather does a high-pressure system bring?
Usually dry, settled weather with little wind. In summer that often means sunshine and warmth; in winter, frost, fog or low cloud can linger under a high.

What pressure is considered high?
Above about 1020 hPa (30.1 inHg) is usually considered high pressure; strong highs reach 1040 hPa (30.7 inHg) or more.

Which way does air turn around a high?
Clockwise in the Northern Hemisphere and counterclockwise in the Southern Hemisphere.
