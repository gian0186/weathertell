Low-Pressure System

An area where atmospheric pressure is lower than its surroundings, typically associated with clouds, rain and unsettled weather.

Air spirals inward and rises within a low-pressure system, cooling as it climbs and condensing into cloud and precipitation. Low-pressure systems are the engines behind most rain, wind and storms in the mid-latitudes, and can range from a mild, passing weather front to an intense storm or hurricane depending on how deep the pressure drop is.

How low is low?

Average sea-level pressure is 1013 hPa (29.92 inHg). A low-pressure system is simply an area where the pressure is lower than around it, so there is no fixed threshold.

Pressure at the centerTypical weather
1000 to 1010 hPa (29.5 to 29.8 inHg)A weak low: clouds and some rain
980 to 1000 hPa (28.9 to 29.5 inHg)A normal autumn or winter storm system, with rain and strong wind
960 to 980 hPa (28.3 to 28.9 inHg)A deep low: a severe storm with damaging gusts
Below 960 hPa (28.3 inHg)An exceptional storm or a strong hurricane
The lowest sea-level pressure ever measured was 870 hPa (25.69 inHg) in Typhoon Tip over the Pacific in October 1979.

Why lows bring clouds and rain

Near the ground, air flows in toward the center of a low and is forced upward. As it rises it cools, and the moisture in it condenses into clouds and rain. In the Northern Hemisphere the air spirals in counterclockwise, in the Southern Hemisphere clockwise, because of the rotation of the Earth. Most lows outside the tropics carry weather fronts with them: a warm front with long periods of rain, followed by a cold front with showers and a drop in temperature.

Wind and the bomb cyclone

The faster the pressure drops over a short distance, the stronger the wind. On a weather map that shows up as closely spaced isobars around the low. When the pressure at the center of a mid-latitude low drops by 24 hPa or more in 24 hours, meteorologists call it explosive cyclogenesis, better known as a bomb cyclone. Such rapidly deepening storms are common off the US East Coast and over the North Atlantic in winter.

Frequently asked questions

What weather does a low-pressure system bring?
Usually clouds, rain or snow, and wind. The deeper the low and the tighter the pressure gradient, the stronger the wind.

What pressure is considered low?
There is no fixed number: a low is lower than its surroundings. As a guide, below about 1000 hPa (29.5 inHg) usually means unsettled weather, and below 980 hPa (28.9 inHg) a real storm.

Which way does air turn around a low?
Counterclockwise in the Northern Hemisphere and clockwise in the Southern Hemisphere.
