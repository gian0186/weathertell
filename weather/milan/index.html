Practical weather notes for Milan

Milan sits in the Po Valley, largely enclosed by the Alps to the north and the Apennines to the south, which traps humidity and heat in summer and fog in the colder months. Summer afternoons can feel more oppressive than the temperature alone suggests, since the surrounding mountains limit the breeze that helps cool coastal Italian cities.

Seasons in Milan

Winters bring the valley's characteristic fog, sometimes lingering for days at a time, along with occasional cold snaps that are sharper than Milan's relatively low latitude might suggest. Spring and autumn tend to be the most changeable seasons, with rain more likely than in the drier Italian south.

Best time to visit Milan

The warmest month in Milan is July, with an average high of 29.9°C (86°F). The coolest is January, at 7.8°C (46°F). The wettest month is May, with about 163 mm (6.4 in) of rain. Highs of 25°C (77°F) or more are typical from June to August. For sightseeing and walking, with highs between 18 and 27°C (64 to 81°F), April, May, September and October are the most comfortable.

Climate FAQ

What is the warmest month in Milan?
July, with an average high of 29.9°C (86°F) and an average low of 20.0°C (68°F) (averages for 2015-2024).

What is the wettest month in Milan?
May, with about 163 mm (6.4 in) of rain. The driest month is January.
