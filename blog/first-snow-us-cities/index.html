When Does It First Snow? New York, Chicago, Boston and More

By Emma Clarke · October 28, 2026

The first snowflakes of the season always make news, but when do they usually arrive? We looked at 36 winters of weather data for 7 major US cities to find the typical date of the first snow, and how early and how late it has come.

Photo: Jazz Guy, Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 2.0)

Key facts
  • Earliest: Denver usually sees its first snow around Oct 10, the earliest of the 7 cities.
  • Chicago: typical first snow around Nov 21; it came before December 1 in 25 of 36 seasons.
  • New York: typical first snow around Dec 6; the earliest was Oct 29, 2011.
  • Washington and Philadelphia: the first snow usually holds off until mid-December.
  • Big spread: in every city the date varies by weeks or even months from one year to the next.

When does the first snow fall? 7 US cities compared

For each season from 1990-91 to 2025-26, we looked for the first day from September 1 with at least 0.5 cm (0.2 in) of snowfall, and took the middle (median) date as the typical first snow.

Denver is in a league of its own: at the foot of the Rocky Mountains, the first snow usually falls in October, and in our data it came before December 1 in almost every season. Chicago typically follows in the second half of November. On the East Coast, Boston and New York usually see their first snow in early December, while Washington, D.C. and Philadelphia more often wait until mid-December or later.

Early surprises and long waits

The spread from year to year is large. In New York, Philadelphia and Washington, D.C., the earliest first snow in our data fell on Oct 29, 2011, when an unusual pre-Halloween storm hit the Northeast. At the other extreme, New York had to wait until Jan 18, 2007 in one season, and Washington, D.C. until Feb 1, 2023. In Denver, the earliest first snow in our data came on Sep 8, 2001, still in summer by the calendar.

What counts as the first snow?

A few flakes mixed with rain often don't settle and may not even be measured. We count the first day with at least 0.5 cm (0.2 in) of snowfall, roughly the first time snow could cover the grass or a car. That is a little stricter than the official "first measurable snow" of 0.1 inch (0.25 cm) used by the National Weather Service, so official records can show a slightly earlier date. Our numbers also come from a weather model for an area around each city, not from a single station, so they can differ from official station records.

What about this winter?

Whether the first snow comes early or late this year depends on the timing of the first cold air outbreaks, which can't be predicted months ahead. The strong El Niño expected for winter 2026-27 tends to favor a milder winter across many northern areas, as we explain in our winter forecast, but that says little about the date of a single snowfall. The best way to know is to watch the 14-day forecast: once nighttime lows near or below freezing show up together with precipitation, the first snow could be close. Frequently asked questions

When does it usually first snow in New York?
In our data for 1990-91 to 2025-26, the typical first snow in New York fell around Dec 6. The earliest was Oct 29, 2011 and the latest Jan 18, 2007.

When does it first snow in Chicago?
Typically around Nov 21. In 25 of 36 seasons, Chicago saw its first snow before December 1.

Which US city gets the first snow earliest?
Of the 7 cities in our comparison, Denver: the typical first snow comes around Oct 10, and in our data the earliest was Sep 8, 2001.

Does El Niño delay the first snow?
Not in a predictable way. A strong El Niño tends to bring a milder winter to many northern areas, but the date of the first snow depends on individual cold spells that can't be forecast months ahead.
